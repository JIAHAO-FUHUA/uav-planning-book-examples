# Sources and scope: Sections 4.3.5--4.3.6

Checked 20 October 2026. These examples are original small mathematical
implementations; no upstream source is bundled or used at runtime.

- Local book: Peter Corke, Robotics, Vision and Control, 3rd ed., 2023,
  Sections 5.2 and 5.4: grid maps, robot extent and obstacle inflation.
  Printed pages 177--181 discuss grid planning/inflation. The goal-distance
  transform there is distinguished from obstacle-distance ESDF queries here.
- Hornung et al., OctoMap, Autonomous Robots 34, 189--206 (2013):
  https://octomap.github.io/ . Occupied/free/unknown distinction and probabilistic
  mapping. Prior p=0.5, increments +0.85/-0.4 and thresholds .65/.35 in our
  example are illustrative choices, not a copy of calibrated OctoMap settings.
- Oleynikova et al., Voxblox, IROS 2017, pp.1366--1373:
  https://helenol.com/publications/iros_2017_voxblox.pdf . ESDF/TSDF distinction
  and incremental distance construction. Our code queries an analytic circle;
  it does not reproduce Voxblox, OctoMap or an incremental grid distance field.
- Wang et al., Geometrically Constrained Trajectory Optimization for
  Multicopters (T-RO 2022), Section IV; see SOURCES_MINCO.md. The coefficient
  gradient for fixed physical sample locations follows the chain rule, and
  reduced waypoint gradients can use the existing transpose solve.

recent active maintenance or real-flight safety.

## Example assumptions and figures

- Figures 4.21--4.23: English Times New Roman, monochrome, editable SVG text
  and geometry; generated by maps_replanning_figures.py. The occupancy ray and
  ideal disk field in Figure 4.21 are different explanatory scenes.
- True static signed distance is positive in free space. A circular obstacle
  is treated as a 2D disk in a fixed-height slice. Gradients are not unique at
  its center; the code rejects that query rather than inventing a direction.
- A sampled squared-hinge cost supplies a descent direction, not a guarantee.
  The interval check requires a valid speed bound, an explicit distance-error
  bound, both endpoints and known space for the entire interval. It is a
  sufficient test: failure is inconclusive. Its proof is the 1-Lipschitz distance
  inequality and the nearest-endpoint time gap of at most half an interval.
- The handoff curve uses MINCO with fixed p/v/a/jerk at its endpoints. Local
  durations 1.4/3.0 s, positions (1.6,0), (2.1,1.3), (5,0), initial velocity
  (1,0), other initial derivatives zero and terminal derivatives zero.
  The new disk center (2.7,0), radius .35 m requires .20 m clearance. A numerical
  stationary-root check gives minimum distance about .23917 m. It is a floating
  point check (imaginary root tolerance 1e-7), not a formal certificate.
- Planning request at 1.0 s, switch at 1.6 s, ready at 1.3 s, scheduling reserve
  .05 s. Validation inputs to admission() are supplied by the caller. A changed
  map returns revalidate-map; only a freshly checked map version may be admitted.
  No actual asynchronous threads, controller, hardware or wall-clock deadline
  bound are implemented. No dynamics-limit validation of this curve is claimed.
- The stopping formula assumes constant speed during latency and constant
  subsequent braking; general jerk-constrained braking needs its own solve.
- The standalone C++ program covers map-query and admission mechanisms; the
  earlier minco.cpp supplies the separate C++ coefficient/gradient example.
